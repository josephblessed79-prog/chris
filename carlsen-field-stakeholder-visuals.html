The Problem

Vulnerability Chain

  • Extreme dependence on imported food — shipping disruptions (COVID, hurricanes, freight costs) expose the population directly
  • Limited arable land under cultivation; youth disengaged from traditional agriculture
  • Tropical heat and humidity constrain open-field production for many high-value crops
  • Post-harvest losses are high without proper cold chain infrastructure
  • Foreign exchange drain from food imports weakens national resilience
  • Climate change intensifies rainfall variability and heat stress
What CEA Can Address

The Opportunity (With Discipline)

  • Year-round production independent of rainfall and seasonal variation
  • Higher yield per acre than open-field for target crops (leafy greens, herbs, seedlings)
  • Reduced post-harvest loss through integrated pack and cold chain
  • Water use 80–95% lower than open-field through recirculation
  • Biosecurity reduces pesticide dependency — cleaner produce for local market
  • Modern technology-adjacent jobs that attract younger workforce
T&T Climate Design Rationale

Why This Specific Design for Tropical Conditions

Heat & Humidity

Wings use natural ventilation, insect screening, shade cloth and fans — passive-first design that works with the climate instead of fighting it. The Spine gets dedicated HVAC/dehumidification sized from actual psychrometric data, not imported specs.

Rainfall & Flooding

Site drainage and stormwater management designed for Trinidad rainfall patterns. Water storage captures rainwater as a resource. Final drainage line subject to hydrology study — not assumed.

Grid Reality

T&TEC grid is primary power. Generator with ATS provides backup. UPS protects controls. Survival Mode keeps irrigation, essential climate, cold chain and alarms running during outages. No cloud dependency for critical functions.

Local Research Foundation

Suraj, Ekwue & Birch (2018) provide directly relevant evidence from Trinidad and Tobago that active climate control can improve greenhouse conditions and plant growth under local climatic conditions. Graamans et al. (2017, 2018) show that plant factories and greenhouses have different energy balances. This means important technical and operating assumptions must be tested locally — not copied from another climate and treated as fact.

Project Phasing & Timeline

Gate-based programme. No jump from idea to full build. No invented calendar promises. Proceed only when gate criteria are met.

0

Stage 0 — Measure & Verify

Pre-Build

  • Detailed surveys: geotechnical, boundary, drainage, levels
  • Confirm planning, approvals, utility connections
  • Water testing and source verification (WASA / well / rain)
  • Local psychrometric data collection for climate sizing
  • Market research: buyers, pricing, demand confirmation
  • De-risk and finalise design assumptions before any spend
1

Stage 1 — Wing 0 Proving Cell

Low Capital

  • Inspect and upgrade existing greenhouse structure (Wing 0)
  • Install full monitoring: climate, water quality, energy metering
  • Test crops, climate management, and operating procedures
  • Train first operators; build maintenance knowledge
  • Collect the evidence base that informs all later stages
2

Stage 2 — Pilot / First Commercial Campus

Primary Investment

  • Build compact Spine (2 levels) + Wing 1 + utility yard
  • Build packhouse and cold chain facility
  • Achieve stable production and first commercial sales
  • Full utilities integration: power, water, fertigation, controls
  • Market integration: buyer relationships, quality standards, pricing
3

Stage 3 — Controlled Pilot Expansion

If Justified

  • Additional modules on pilot site only if demand and economics justify
  • No second commercial Wing assumed in base case
  • Expand operating team and training programme
4

Stage 4 — Conditional Full-Scale Campus

Subject to All Gates

  • Pursue State land allocation only with proven operating data
  • Requires: tenure + planning + EMA + finance + demand
  • Replicate proven modules from pilot; four Wings (A–D) around central Spine
  • Admin/training centre, larger packhouse, dedicated utilities
  • Regional replication potential from demonstrated model

Environmental Stewardship

Responsible resource use by design, not afterthought. Every system measured and optimised.

💧

Water

  • 80–95% less water than open-field through recirculating hydroponics
  • Rainwater harvesting from roof and greenhouse structures
  • Isolated nutrient loops — no cross-contamination between zones
  • Treated discharge to approved standard; responsible reuse where possible
  • Sub-metered per zone for continuous monitoring
⚡

Energy

  • Wings use natural light — no artificial lighting energy cost for sunlight crops
  • LED production concentrated in Spine where it earns its energy cost
  • Roof/carport solar PV as supplemental (not primary)
  • Passive-first climate strategy for Wings: ventilation, shade, fans before cooling
  • Sub-metered by zone, tracked in SCADA historian
♻️

Waste & Land

  • Organic waste composted or sent to approved reuse/disposal
  • No pesticide/herbicide runoff — biosecure growing environment
  • Stormwater management designed for Trinidad rainfall patterns
  • Higher productivity per acre reduces land pressure
  • Waste routing separated from product flow (biosecurity principle)

Environmental, social and community considerations will be addressed in accordance with T&T laws and best practice. All drainage, discharge and environmental impacts are subject to EMA and relevant authority approval.

Risk Landscape & Mitigations

Every risk is addressed by the gate-based approach. No commitment without evidence. The design philosophy: reduce exposure before it becomes irreversible.

Energy Cost & Grid Reliability

High Impact

Risk: T&TEC tariff increases or extended outages could threaten operating economics.

Mitigation: Passive-first Wing design minimises energy demand. Generator + UPS provides backup. Sub-metering tracks every kWh. Solar PV reduces grid dependency. Survival Mode protects critical loads.

State Land Tenure

High Impact

Risk: Full-scale campus depends on State land allocation that may not materialise.

Mitigation: Pilot is entirely on private land. State land is Stage 4 only. The pilot must prove viability independently. No capital at risk from tenure uncertainty.

Market Demand & Pricing

Medium Impact

Risk: Local market may not absorb production at viable prices.

Mitigation: Market validation is a Stage 0 gate requirement. First sales happen at Stage 2 before scaling. Multiple channels (retail, institutional, hospitality). Scale only when demand is proven.

Climate Performance

Medium Impact

Risk: Imported CEA assumptions may not hold in Trinidad's heat and humidity.

Mitigation: Wing 0 proving cell tests everything locally before building Wing 1. Psychrometric data drives HVAC sizing. No assumptions copied from temperate climates unchecked.

Supply Chain for Spares

Medium Impact

Risk: Specialised components may be hard to source in Trinidad.

Mitigation: Design philosophy uses mature, widely-available technology. Manual fallback for critical functions. No proprietary lock-in. Local technician maintainability is a design requirement.

Water Source Reliability

Low-Medium

Risk: WASA supply interruptions or well yield uncertainty.

Mitigation: Water testing and source verification is Stage 0 gate. On-site storage sized for interruptions. Rainwater harvesting supplements supply. Recirculation minimises total demand.

Why Hybrid CEA?

Comparing approaches: the Carlsen Field hybrid model vs. full indoor vertical farm vs. open-field agriculture.

Factor Carlsen Field Hybrid Full Indoor Vertical Farm Open-Field Agriculture
Capital Cost Moderate — Wings use natural light; Spine is compact Very High — Full artificial lighting, sealed environment Low — Minimal infrastructure
Energy Use Low-Moderate — Sunlight crops in Wings; LED only where justified Very High — 100% artificial light, full HVAC Very Low — Sun and rain only
Yield per Acre High — Protected environment, year-round production Very High — Stacked growing, 365 days Low — Seasonal, weather dependent
Water Efficiency 80–95% less than open-field 90–98% less than open-field Baseline — Irrigation losses, runoff
Tropical Suitability Designed for T&T — passive-first climate strategy Energy penalty — massive cooling load in tropics Native but exposed to storms, pests, flooding
Maintainability Local technicians — mature, serviceable technology Specialised staff — proprietary systems common Traditional skills — widely available
Biosecurity Strong — insect screen, isolation, dirty-clean separation Very Strong — sealed environment Weak — open to pests, disease, weather
Risk to T&T Low — staged, gate-based, proven tech High — large upfront bet, unproven in local conditions Low but limited upside for food security

The hybrid model captures 80% of the benefits of indoor farming at a fraction of the energy cost and capital risk — designed specifically for Trinidad's climate, grid reality and maintenance capacity.

The Complete Pitch

Everything a stakeholder needs to understand in one view.

“From Local Roots to a More Secure Tomorrow”

Carlsen Field is a controlled environment agriculture programme for Trinidad and Tobago that builds food security through staged, evidence-based investment — proving what works locally before scaling.

Six Things Every Stakeholder Should Know

  1. Food security starts with evidence, not ambition. Every material assumption is measured and verified before capital is committed.
  2. Private site proves, State site scales. No taxpayer money at risk during the proving phase. State land is requested only after commercial viability is demonstrated.
  3. Mature technology, smarter architecture. The innovation is in the system design, not in unproven tech. Complexity earns its place or it does not get in.
  4. Designed for Trinidad. Heat, humidity, rainfall, grid reality, local technicians and FX/spares all considered. Not a temperate-climate blueprint copy.
  5. Jobs and capability. Operator, maintenance, food handling, data and management skills — built into the programme from Stage 1.
  6. The value is replicability. Not one beautiful farm, but a proven module that can be repeated responsibly — across Trinidad and potentially the Caribbean.
The Core Principle

Use mature, serviceable technology in a smarter architecture. Complexity must earn its place.
