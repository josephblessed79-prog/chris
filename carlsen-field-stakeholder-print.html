Scale-Site Conditions

This campus is conditional. It proceeds only when all of the following are satisfied:

  • Pilot site demonstrates commercial viability with measured data
  • State land tenure secured through Commissioner of State Lands
  • EMA Certificate of Environmental Clearance obtained
  • DevelopTT planning permission granted
  • Finance secured against demonstrated (not projected) returns
  • Market demand confirmed at scale volumes
  • Operating team capable of managing expanded campus
Replication Principle
  • Same Spine architecture — do not redesign what is proven
  • Wings replicate proven module; each independently isolated
  • Expanded packhouse and cold chain sized to actual demand
  • Admin and training centre enables workforce development
  • Stormwater management per hydrological study
  • Future reserve land kept for expansion only if justified

Layout, land use, access routes and zone sizes are indicative. Subject to state land allocation process, environmental assessment, planning conditions, utility capacity studies and detailed engineering design. No area, dimension or position is committed.

S05 / The Compact Spine
Two Occupied Levels + Roof Plant
The Spine is the technical core of the campus. It houses the functions that require the most control — nursery propagation, compact LED leafy production, operations and controls — stacked vertically to reduce footprint. Roof level carries HVAC, dehumidification and extraction plant. This is a concept section — no dimensions, structural sizes or equipment specifications are committed.
Concept section — NTS — no structural design, dimensions or equipment sizes committed

Power

T&TEC grid primary. Generator backup with ATS. UPS for controls. Sizes TBD from load study.

Water

WASA / well / rain — sources to be tested. Treatment as required. Isolated nutrient loops per zone.

Climate

Wings: passive first (ventilation, shade, fans). Spine: dedicated HVAC sized from measured data.

Controls

PLC/HMI local operation. SCADA historian for data. Sub-metering all zones. Manual fallback.

Survival Mode

Grid loss: UPS holds, generator starts, critical loads only. No cloud dependency for essential functions.

S06 / Biosecurity and Movement
Five Flows — The Butterfly Logic
The Butterfly is a process flow concept, not a building shape. It describes how five distinct flow categories move through the campus from dirty side to clean side, with controlled transitions. Every flow is managed to prevent contamination crossing from one zone to another.

1. People Flow

All personnel transition through hygiene zone before entering production. Visitors restricted. Movement logged. Clean-side clothing only in production areas.

2. Input Flow

All materials received at service yard (dirty side). Inspected, decontaminated and repackaged before transfer to clean storage. No direct path from external to production.

3. Harvest Flow

Product moves from production to packhouse through clean transfer. Graded, packed and stored at crop-appropriate temperatures. Basil above approximately 12 °C (chilling-sensitive). Dispatch from clean side only.

4. Waste Flow

Organic waste, spent nutrient solution, and packaging routed out of production through dedicated waste path. Never crosses product flow. Exits via service yard for approved disposal or composting.

5. Maintenance Flow

Technicians and tools enter through decontamination. Access to service corridors, utility risers and roof plant via controlled routes. Maintenance activity logged. Tools cleaned on entry and exit.

The five flows are a design principle. Detailed SOPs, access protocols and physical barriers will be developed during detailed design with reference to food safety and biosecurity best practice.

S07 / Module Isolation
Keep Failure Inside One Module
Each production module (wing or Spine zone) has its own nutrient mixing, crop return and isolation boundary. No shared untreated return lines. If a disease, contamination or system failure occurs in one module, it does not propagate to others. This is the most important design principle in the entire programme.
Why Isolation Matters
  • Disease containment: Root-zone pathogens (Pythium, Fusarium) cannot spread between modules through shared nutrient lines
  • Contamination control: A chemical error in one module's mixing does not poison other crops
  • Failure isolation: A pump failure, leak or sensor fault affects only one module
  • Independent diagnostics: Each module's nutrient, pH, EC and flow data is self-contained — easier to identify and fix problems
  • Staged commissioning: Modules can be commissioned, tested and adjusted independently
What Each Module Contains
  • Own nutrient stock tanks and dosing (A/B concentrate + pH)
  • Own mixing and delivery system
  • Own growing system (NFT, DWC, drip — type per crop)
  • Own return collection and recirculation
  • Own drain-to-waste or treated discharge path
  • Own sub-metering: water, nutrient, energy, climate

Fresh water is the only shared input. Everything downstream of the mixing point is module-specific. This is non-negotiable.

S08 / Phasing and Investment Gates
Stages 0–4 — No Jump from Idea to Full Build
Gate-based programme. No calendar promises. Proceed only when gate criteria are met with measured evidence. Each stage has explicit pass/fail criteria; the outcome at every gate is Go, Redesign, Defer, or No-Go.
0

Stage 0 — Measure and Verify Pre-Build

Activities:

  • Cadastral survey, geotechnical investigation, drainage study
  • Confirm planning path (DevelopTT), utility connections (T&TEC, WASA)
  • Water quality testing, source verification
  • On-site psychrometric data collection (minimum 12 months)
  • Market research: buyers, pricing, demand confirmation

Gate 0 Criteria:

  • Site boundaries and levels confirmed
  • Planning pathway clear
  • Water source viable
  • Climate data sufficient for HVAC concept
  • At least one buyer engaged
  • Investment case supportable
1

Stage 1 — Wing 0 Proving Cell Low Capital

Activities:

  • Assess and retrofit existing structure (approximately 279 m², subject to survey)
  • Install full monitoring: climate, water quality, energy metering
  • Test crops, climate management, operating procedures
  • Train first operators; build maintenance knowledge

Gate 1 Criteria:

  • Crop yield data from local conditions
  • Energy and water consumption measured
  • Operating cost baseline established
  • Team trained and competent
  • System reliability demonstrated
2

Stage 2 — Pilot / First Commercial Campus Primary Investment

Activities:

  • Build compact Spine (2 levels + roof) and Wing 1
  • Packhouse and cold chain facility
  • Full utilities: power, water, fertigation, controls
  • First commercial sales and market integration

Gate 2 Criteria:

  • Stable production at target quality
  • Buyer relationships established
  • Unit economics measured (not projected)
  • Uptime and reliability data
  • Maintenance costs tracked
3

Stage 3 — Controlled Pilot Expansion If Justified

Additional modules on pilot site only if demand and economics justify. No second commercial wing assumed in base case. Expand operating team and training programme. Decision driven by measured margins, not projections.

4

Stage 4 — Conditional Full-Scale Campus Subject to All Gates

State land allocation pursued only with proven operating data. Requires: tenure (Commissioner of State Lands) + planning (DevelopTT) + environmental (EMA CEC) + finance + demand. Replicate proven modules; four Wings (A–D) around central Spine. Regional replication potential from demonstrated model.

The Evidence Chain

Purpose: Reduce exposure to unresolved material assumptions before commitment. Make the reason for each gate decision traceable and defensible.

Crop Selection Principle

The buyer decides what we grow. Measured margins decide if it is viable.

  • No crop is committed until buyer demand and pricing are confirmed
  • Production cost must be measured in Wing 0 / Stage 1 before commercial commitment
  • Yield data from local conditions, not imported assumptions
  • Post-harvest handling is crop-specific — no universal cold chain temperature
  • Crop mix may change over time as market and margins evolve

S10 / Investment Risks
What the Investment Must Survive
Every risk is addressed by the gate-based approach. No commitment without evidence. The design philosophy: reduce exposure to unresolved material assumptions before capital is committed. These risks are acknowledged, not dismissed.

Energy Cost and Grid Reliability

High Impact

Risk: T&TEC tariff changes or extended outages could undermine operating economics. Generator fuel costs are volatile.

Mitigation: Passive-first Wing design minimises energy demand. Generator + UPS provides backup. Sub-metering tracks every kWh. Solar PV reduces grid dependency. Survival Mode protects critical loads. Actual energy costs measured in Stage 1 before scaling.

State Land Tenure

High Impact

Risk: Full-scale campus depends on state land allocation through Commissioner of State Lands — process may be slow, conditional or unsuccessful.

Mitigation: Pilot is entirely on private land. State land is Stage 4 only. The pilot must prove viability independently. No capital is at risk from tenure uncertainty.

Market Demand and Pricing

Medium Impact

Risk: Local market may not absorb production at economically viable prices. Competition from imports or other producers.

Mitigation: Market validation is a Stage 0 gate requirement. First commercial sales at Stage 2 before scaling. Multiple channels. Scale only when demand is measured, not assumed.

Climate Performance Gap

Medium Impact

Risk: Imported CEA assumptions may not hold under Trinidad's heat, humidity and rainfall. Equipment sized from temperate-climate data may underperform.

Mitigation: Wing 0 proving cell tests everything locally before building Wing 1. On-site psychrometric data drives HVAC sizing — no imported specifications used unchecked. UWI research supports local evidence base.

Supply Chain for Spares and Consumables

Medium Impact

Risk: Specialised components may be hard to source in Trinidad. Lead times and FX costs for imported parts.

Mitigation: Design uses mature, widely available technology. Manual fallback for critical functions. No proprietary lock-in. Local technician maintainability is a design requirement.

Regulatory and Approval Risk

Medium Impact

Risk: Planning (DevelopTT), environmental (EMA CEC), utility approvals, and food safety certification timelines are uncertain.

Mitigation: Pre-application enquiries at Stage 0 before capital commitment. No construction starts without required approvals. Regulatory pathway is a gate criterion.

Water Source Reliability

Low–Medium Impact

Risk: WASA supply interruptions; well yield may be insufficient or water quality unsuitable.

Mitigation: Water testing and source verification is Stage 0 gate. On-site storage sized for supply interruptions. Rainwater harvesting supplements supply. Recirculating hydroponics minimises total consumption.

Workforce and Skills

Low–Medium Impact

Risk: Skilled greenhouse operators, maintenance technicians and data-literate staff may be scarce locally.

Mitigation: Training built into the programme from Stage 1. Wing 0 is the training ground. Standard operating procedures developed and refined before scaling. Technology chosen for local maintainability.

The gate-based approach does not eliminate risk. It ensures that each risk is identified, measured where possible, and mitigated before the capital associated with it is committed.

S11 / Stakeholder Conversation
Who to Talk to and What to Seek
Different stakeholders need different emphasis. Same programme, same evidence standard — tailored message. This page maps who is involved and what each conversation should address.
Government / Ministry of Agriculture

What They Care About

  • Reducing food import dependency and FX drain
  • National food security and resilience
  • Job creation and skills development
  • Visible, replicable success story

What to Seek

Policy alignment confirmation. Access to agricultural incentives. Introduction to state land allocation process. Support for SIDS food security agenda.

Commissioner of State Lands / DevelopTT

What They Care About

  • Productive use of state land
  • Proven operator, not speculative proposal
  • Environmental compliance and community benefit

What to Seek

State land allocation pathway. Planning permission requirements and timeline. Pre-application guidance. Understanding of conditions and obligations.

Financial Institutions / Investors

What They Care About

  • Clear business model and unit economics
  • Risk mitigation through staged investment
  • Proven technology, not experimental
  • Collateral and exit strategy

What to Seek

Staged financing aligned to gate outcomes. Agricultural development loans or incentives. Investment structured around measured (not projected) returns.

EMA / Environmental

What They Care About

  • Environmental impact assessment
  • Water discharge and drainage compliance
  • Waste management plan
  • Noise, dust, traffic during construction

What to Seek

Certificate of Environmental Clearance (CEC) requirements. Pre-application guidance. Understanding of conditions for agricultural facilities in this area.

T&TEC / Govt Electrical Inspectorate

T&TEC (Supply)

  • Tariff structure and phased demand growth

Govt Electrical Inspectorate (Certification)

  • Installation certification and inspection

WASA / Water Authority

What They Care About

Technical Partners / Engineers

Structural, mechanical, electrical engineering services. Local experience with agricultural and industrial buildings. Willingness to design from measured data, not imported assumptions.

Local Community / Carlsen Field

  • Environmental impact, traffic, noise
  • Access to fresh produce

What to Seek

Community support and understanding. Local recruitment pipeline. Feedback on concerns. This is an agricultural campus built responsibly in the community, not a factory.

Research / UWI / Knowledge Partners

What They Care About

  • Applied research opportunities in tropical CEA
  • Data sharing and publication
  • Student projects and training

What to Seek

Research collaboration on climate performance, crop trials, energy optimisation. Access to existing tropical greenhouse research (Suraj, Ekwue and Birch; Graamans et al.). Student placement programme.

Six Things Every Stakeholder Should Know
  1. Food security starts with evidence, not ambition. Every material assumption is measured and verified before capital is committed.
  2. Private site proves, state site scales. No taxpayer money at risk during the proving phase.
  3. Mature technology, smarter architecture. The innovation is in the system design, not in unproven tech.

  5. Jobs and capability. Operator, maintenance, data and management skills built into the programme.
  6. The value is replicability. A proven module that can be repeated responsibly.
